# Curb demo — voiceover script v1 (8 beats, ~420 words ≈ 3:05 at 140–145 wpm)

For you to record in Clipchamp as soon as this plan is approved. Rules it follows:
- It never speaks a number that will change (row counts, tallies); those appear on screen from live captures.
- It's date-neutral.
- The first words of each beat are its alignment anchor, so read them exactly.
- `⏸` marks a breath, and each one is a cut point.
- Beat 7 has two variants: record both, and the one that matches what's live on mainnet is used.

Recording: one continuous take, about one second of silence between beats. If you fluff a line, pause two seconds
and repeat the whole sentence (or cut the fluff in Clipchamp so no line appears twice). Export as .mp4/.m4a/.wav.

**1 · 11:55 (0:00–0:14)** — *Picture: the live hero flips from paper to ink.*
> At eleven fifty-five in Hong Kong, the primary market for Tencent shuts for lunch. ⏸ On X Layer, its
> tokenized share keeps trading.

**2 · The week (0:14–0:40)** — *Picture: the unroll; 141 h 20 m of 168 on screen.*
> That isn't a glitch. When the exchange is shut, the issuer caps creation and redemption at zero, so
> nothing pulls the token back to the real share. ⏸ For a Hong Kong stock, that's more than a hundred and
> forty hours of every week. ⏸ Lenders can't value those hours, and holders can't get out without
> selling into a thin pool.

**3 · The name (0:40–0:52)** — *Picture: type card; the mark resolves.*
> A century ago, New York's Curb Market traded outdoors, on the street. ⏸ Curb is that market for the
> hours the exchange is shut.

**4 · MarketClock (0:52–1:24)** — *Picture: /clock; an OKLink attestation; the terminal.*
> Curb starts with MarketClock: a free, on-chain oracle that says whether each stock's home market is
> really open. ⏸ One host attests it every few minutes, a second, independent host checks and signs every
> round, and each round commits the exact issuer data it was built from, so anyone can re-derive it
> with one command. ⏸ And every transaction
> carries our X Layer Builder Code.

**5 · Scorecard (1:24–1:56)** — anchor: "Before a reopen" — *Picture: a row committed, then graded; the terminal verifying it.*
> Before a reopen, Curb commits its price on chain. ⏸ After the reopen, the contract reads the pool
> itself and grades us against the last price. Nobody supplies the answer, not even us. ⏸ When the
> pools went quiet, our marks tied, and the record says so, because a tie isn't a win. ⏸ So we changed
> the method, not the rows: the new mark listens to markets that trade while Hong Kong is shut.

**6 · Agents pay (1:56–2:32)** — *Picture: marketplace #13869; the 402; onchainos paying; the receipt.*
> Agents can buy all of this. Curb is registered on OKX's AI marketplace, with three paid services
> over x402. ⏸ Ask without paying, and you get a free preview and a price. ⏸ Pay one cent from an OKX Agentic
> Wallet, and you get the answer and a receipt that ties the payment to the exact bytes you received.
> ⏸ This one is our own wallet paying us: it proves the rail, not demand.

**7A · Instruments — live (2:32–2:58)** — *Picture: /notes certificate, auction; /depth LTV.*
> If you need out while Hong Kong is shut, you don't have to dump the stock. ⏸ Sell a Reopen Note, a
> claim that settles at the verified reopen, through a falling-price auction. ⏸ And market makers can
> bond the depth they already provide; if they fade a fill, anyone can prove it, and the bond goes to the
> trader.

**7B · Instruments — in build (2:32–2:58)** — *Picture: the specimen certificate, the LTV curve.*
> If you need out while Hong Kong is shut, you shouldn't have to dump the stock. ⏸ We're building that
> next, on the same clock: a Reopen Note that settles at the verified reopen, and bonded market-maker

**8 · Close (2:58–3:12)** — *Picture: the live hero; end card.*
> The exchange keeps its hours. The market doesn't. ⏸ Curb: the market that trades when the exchange is
